<h2 class="text-xl font-semibold text-foreground mt-10 mb-3">Definition: What is a semi truck preventive maintenance schedule?</h2>
<p class="text-base leading-relaxed"><strong>Preventive maintenance (PM) definition:</strong> A semi truck preventive maintenance schedule is a structured, interval-based service plan — organized by miles, engine hours, or calendar time — that inspects, adjusts, and replaces wear items before they fail, keeping commercial vehicles compliant, reliable, and profitable.</p>
<p class="text-base leading-relaxed">Before structured PM programs, most fleets ran trucks until something broke. That reactive approach worked when trucks were simpler and freight margins were wider. Today, with EGR systems, DPF filters, air ride suspensions, and FMCSA compliance requirements stacked on top of each other, waiting for a failure is a fast way to go from a minor repair to a major one — or to get parked at a weigh station because a brake adjuster is out of spec.</p>

<h2 class="text-xl font-semibold text-foreground mt-10 mb-3">How a fleet PM schedule works: From pre-trip to annual inspection</h2>
<p class="text-base leading-relaxed">A solid fleet PM schedule is not a single event — it is a layered system where daily driver checks catch the obvious stuff, short-interval services catch wear before it becomes failure, and annual DOT inspections confirm everything is compliant. Here is how each layer stacks up for a Phoenix-area commercial vehicle operation.</p>
<ul class="space-y-3 my-4 list-none p-0">
  <li class="flex gap-3 p-4 rounded-xl border border-gray-200 bg-white">
    <span class="text-lg font-bold flex-shrink-0" style="color: #DC2626;">01</span>
    <div><h4 class="font-semibold text-foreground text-sm mb-1 mt-0">Daily Pre-Trip Inspection (Driver-Performed)</h4>
    <p class="text-sm leading-relaxed mb-0">Check fluid levels, tire pressure and condition, lights, mirrors, brakes, and coupling devices before every run. In Phoenix summer, add a visual check on radiator hoses and coolant reservoir level — heat stress makes hose failures more common at load time.</p></div>
  </li>
  <li class="flex gap-3 p-4 rounded-xl border border-gray-200 bg-white">
    <span class="text-lg font-bold flex-shrink-0" style="color: #DC2626;">02</span>
    <div><h4 class="font-semibold text-foreground text-sm mb-1 mt-0">PM-A Service (Every 15,000–25,000 Miles)</h4>
    <p class="text-sm leading-relaxed mb-0">Engine oil and filter change, fuel filter inspection, grease fittings lubricated, belts and hoses inspected, air filter checked, tire rotation and pressure set, brake adjustment checked. This is the core rhythm of any fleet PM schedule for semi trucks in Arizona.</p></div>
  </li>
  <li class="flex gap-3 p-4 rounded-xl border border-gray-200 bg-white">
    <span class="text-lg font-bold flex-shrink-0" style="color: #DC2626;">03</span>
    <div><h4 class="font-semibold text-foreground text-sm mb-1 mt-0">PM-B Service (Every 50,000 Miles)</h4>
    <p class="text-sm leading-relaxed mb-0">Everything in PM-A plus coolant system test, full brake inspection including drum measurement and slack adjuster stroke, transmission fluid check, DPF soot load check, and wheel seal inspection. Catching a cracked drum at 50K costs a fraction of what a brake failure costs on I-17.</p></div>
  </li>
  <li class="flex gap-3 p-4 rounded-xl border border-gray-200 bg-white">
    <span class="text-lg font-bold flex-shrink-0" style="color: #DC2626;">04</span>
    <div><h4 class="font-semibold text-foreground text-sm mb-1 mt-0">PM-C Service (Every 100,000 Miles)</h4>
    <p class="text-sm leading-relaxed mb-0">Full coolant flush and refill, injector performance check, EGR and DPF cleaning or replacement evaluation, complete electrical system scan for active and pending fault codes, full suspension inspection including air bags and u-bolts, and steering component wear measurement.</p></div>
  </li>
  <li class="flex gap-3 p-4 rounded-xl border border-gray-200 bg-white">
    <span class="text-lg font-bold flex-shrink-0" style="color: #DC2626;">05</span>
    <div><h4 class="font-semibold text-foreground text-sm mb-1 mt-0">Annual DOT Inspection (Every 12 Months — Required by Federal Law)</h4>
    <p class="text-sm leading-relaxed mb-0">A certified Federal Annual Inspection covering all 49 CFR Part 396 items — brakes, lights, steering, tires, coupling devices, exhaust, fuel system, and frame. CT Shop is a certified inspection station; when you pass, you get the sticker on the spot. Align this with your PM-C when mileage allows to minimize time off the road.</p></div>
  </li>
</ul>

<h2 class="text-xl font-semibold text-foreground mt-10 mb-3">Where the savings actually come from</h2>
<p class="text-base leading-relaxed">Fleet managers sometimes struggle to justify PM costs to ownership until they map out exactly where the money is going. The savings from a disciplined truck maintenance interval program show up in four specific places — and they are not small numbers.</p>
<ul class="list-disc pl-6 space-y-1.5 text-base leading-relaxed">
  <li><strong>Avoided towing and emergency labor:</strong> A single after-hours roadside call with wrecker recovery can cost more than two full PM-B services. Eliminating two of those a year pays for the entire PM program on one truck.</li>
  <li><strong>Extended component life:</strong> Properly adjusted brakes last 30–50% longer than neglected ones. Coolant that is flushed on schedule does not corrode water pump impellers or clog radiator cores. These are four- and five-figure repair bills avoided.</li>
  <li><strong>Fuel efficiency:</strong> Under-inflated tires, dirty air filters, and out-of-spec injectors each rob 1–3% of fuel economy. On a truck running 120,000 miles per year at current diesel prices, that adds up fast.</li>
  <li><strong>CSA score management:</strong> Every brake, light, or tire violation at a roadside inspection adds points to your SMS score. Enough points and FMCSA puts you under investigation. A clean <a class="text-primary hover:underline font-medium" style="color: #DC2626;" href="/dot-inspections-phoenix-az">DOT inspection record</a> starts with PM discipline, not luck.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="text-xl font-semibold text-foreground mt-10 mb-3">Common mistakes: Why some fleets still end up on the shoulder</h2>
<p class="text-base leading-relaxed">Even fleets that understand the value of PM make process errors that undermine the whole program. These are the patterns we see most often at the shop when a truck rolls in with something that should have been caught weeks earlier.</p>
<ul class="list-disc pl-6 space-y-2 text-base leading-relaxed">
  <li><strong>Mileage-only scheduling.</strong> Trucks in Phoenix often idle heavily for AC and refrigeration — engine hours accumulate even when miles do not. If your interval is miles-only, you are missing wear that is building on the clock. Build engine-hour triggers into the schedule alongside mileage.</li>
  <li><strong>Skipping trailer PM.</strong> The tractor gets serviced; the trailer gets forgotten. Trailer brake drums, slack adjusters, ABS sensors, and lighting harnesses all wear on their own schedule. A tractor that is perfectly maintained can still get parked at a scale for a <a class="text-primary hover:underline font-medium" style="color: #DC2626;" href="/semi-trailer-brake-and-tire-repair-services-in-phoenix-az">trailer brake defect</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>Ignoring fault codes until something visible breaks.</strong> An amber check-engine light on a Cummins or Detroit does not always feel urgent. But a stored EGR or DPF fault code left unaddressed can force a truck into derate mode on a loaded run across I-10 in the summer. Pull codes at every PM interval.</li>
  <li><strong>Using the wrong oil spec for desert heat.</strong> Not all engine oils are equal in sustained 115-degree ambient temps. Confirm your Cummins, Detroit, Cat, or Volvo engine spec and run the viscosity the OEM recommends for high-heat operation — not whatever was on sale at the parts house.</li>
  <li><strong>No documentation trail.</strong> If you cannot produce maintenance records during a <a class="text-primary hover:underline font-medium" style="color: #DC2626;" href="/dot-inspections-phoenix-az">DOT audit</a>, the work might as well not have happened from a compliance standpoint. Every PM service needs a dated work order with mileage, parts used, and tech signature — keep at least 12 months on file per vehicle.</li>
</ul>

<div class="my-6 p-5 rounded-xl border border-amber-500/30 bg-amber-50">
  <p class="text-sm font-bold text-amber-800 mb-2">Watch out</p>
  <p class="text-sm leading-relaxed text-foreground mb-0">Phoenix dust is abrasive and gets into everything — air filter restriction gauges can read fine visually while a filter is already at near-capacity because fine desert particulate packs densely without looking plugged. A restricted air filter starves the engine of combustion air, raises EGTs, and accelerates turbo wear fast. Check restriction gauge readings under load, not just at idle in the yard, and replace air filters on a calendar interval in addition to a restriction trigger. At CT Shop, every PM service includes a restriction check as part of the standard intake inspection.</p>
</div>

<h2 class="text-xl font-semibold text-foreground mt-10 mb-3">Pro strategies: Get more from your semi truck PM program</h2>

<h3 class="text-lg font-semibold text-foreground mt-6 mb-2">Run diagnostics at every B-service interval, not just when a light is on</h3>
<p class="text-base leading-relaxed">Modern semi trucks store pending fault codes that have not yet triggered a dashboard warning. These are early warning signals — the kind that give you a window to fix something on your schedule rather than the road's schedule. Plugging into the ECM at every 50,000-mile service catches those codes while the truck is already in the bay. Our <a class="text-primary hover:underline font-medium" style="color: #DC2626;" href="/semi-truck-diagnostics-in-phoenix-az">semi truck diagnostics</a> service covers full fault code retrieval and interpretation across Cummins, Detroit, Cat, Volvo, Mack, International, Freightliner, Peterbilt, and Kenworth platforms.</p>
<h3 class="text-lg font-semibold text-foreground mt-6 mb-2">Build AC service into your spring PM cycle</h3>
<p class="text-base leading-relaxed">In Phoenix, cab AC is not a comfort item — it is a safety system. A driver running I-10 in July with a marginal compressor is a heat-stress incident waiting to happen. Service your AC system in March or April before the heat arrives, not in July when compressor demand is already maxing out every day. <h2 class="text-xl font-semibold text-foreground mt-10 mb-3">Frequently asked questions about semi truck preventive maintenance</h2>
<div class="space-y-4 my-4">
  <div class="rounded-xl border border-gray-200 bg-white p-4">
    <p class="font-semibold text-foreground text-sm mb-1.5 mt-0">How often should a semi truck get an oil change in Arizona?</p>
    <p class="text-sm leading-relaxed mb-0">Most modern heavy-duty diesels run 15,000 to 25,000 mile oil change intervals depending on the engine OEM spec, oil type, and duty cycle. In Phoenix's sustained heat, if your truck idles heavily for AC load or refrigeration units, use engine hours as a secondary trigger — roughly 250 to 400 hours depending on the OEM. Always follow the engine manufacturer's spec, not a generic one-size-fits-all interval.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="rounded-xl border border-gray-200 bg-white p-4">
    <p class="font-semibold text-foreground text-sm mb-1.5 mt-0">What does a DOT annual inspection cover and who can perform it?</p>
    <p class="text-sm leading-relaxed mb-0">A federal annual inspection under 49 CFR Part 396 covers brake systems, steering, lighting, tires, wheels and rims, coupling devices, exhaust, fuel system, frame, and suspension. It must be performed by a qualified inspector at a certified facility.   </div>
  <div class="rounded-xl border border-gray-200 bg-white p-4">
    <p class="font-semibold text-foreground text-sm mb-1.5 mt-0">How do I know when my DPF needs cleaning versus replacement?</p>
    <p class="text-sm leading-relaxed mb-0">The truck's ECM tracks soot and ash load in the DPF. Soot regenerates on its own or through active regen cycles — ash does not and must be cleaned out by forced-air cleaning at a shop. If your truck is going into regen more frequently than normal, taking longer to complete regen, or throwing a high-backpressure fault code, it is time for a DPF inspection. We check DPF soot load as part of every PM-B service.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="rounded-xl border border-gray-200 bg-white p-4">
    <p class="font-semibold text-foreground text-sm mb-1.5 mt-0">What is the biggest brake mistake fleets make that gets trucks put out of service?</p>
    <p class="text-sm leading-relaxed mb-0">Brake pushrod stroke out of adjustment is the most common brake OOS violation at roadside inspections — <a href="https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/sites/fmcsa.dot.gov/files/docs/2017-CVSA-OOS-Criteria_Final-1.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener" class="underline">CVSA criteria</a> are strict on this and inspectors measure it directly. Automatic slack adjusters can give a false sense of security because they self-adjust within a range — but a worn clevis pin, cracked brake shoe, or dragging brake can push stroke past the limit even with a functional ASA. Manual stroke checks at every PM are not optional.</p>
  </div>
